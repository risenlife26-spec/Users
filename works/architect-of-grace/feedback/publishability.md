# Publishability Pass — *The Architect of Grace*

*Scope: Chapter 1 partial. This pass surfaces blockers — legal,

## Highest-priority blocker: the OMC (motorcycle club) content

**Vagos is named in Chapter 1.** "the bar in Oildale where he'd been
spending Tuesday nights since the Vagos thing settled down."

The Vagos Motorcycle Club is a real, federally-prosecuted OMC. Per
legal playbook §12 ("Outlaw motorcycle club content"), real OMCs are
among the most litigious trademark plaintiffs in the United States,
and even passing references that imply criminal association can
trigger trademark or defamation exposure. The *Rogers v. Grimaldi* /
*Mattel v. MCA* artistic-relevance defense likely wins on the merits
but defense costs are substantial.

**Action required before submission:** invent a fictional OMC for any
plot involvement. Replace "Vagos" with the fictional club name. If a
real OMC must be referenced (e.g., as historical context), keep it
neutral and out of any criminal context.

This is the single largest legal exposure in the chapter.

## Second-priority blocker: identifiable real persons

Per legal playbook §12 (*Bindrim v. Mitchell*), name changes alone do
not protect against defamation suits when professional context,
geographic context, and distinctive behavior make a real person
identifiable.

Characters in Chapter 1 to audit:
- **Mickey** — Vietnam vet, Teamster, "connections to Oakland and
  Newark," Bakersfield-based, brother of Diane. If a real uncle
  matches this profile and is alive, *Bindrim* exposure is real.
  **Composite him.** The legal playbook's recommended technique is
  to draw from multiple real people so no single person can credibly
  claim to be the target. The author has already done this with Cody
  per the planning conversation; extend it deliberately to Mickey
  and any other primary antagonist-source.
- **Diane** — title-company worker on Brundage Lane, Church of
  Christ on Union Avenue. The specifics (employer category, exact
  street, exact church) make her potentially identifiable. If based
  on a real mother, consider varying the employer category and the
  street (keep Bakersfield, change the specifics).
- **Cody** — composite per author. Confirm.
- **Patricia** (Cody's mother) — Delano, packing-shed worker,
  "hunted quietness." If based on a real person, audit.

**Action required:** before any agent or beta-reader sees the
manuscript, complete a real-person audit. Document the composite
sources for each character (kept privately, not in the manuscript).
Per playbook §12, this documentation reduces exposure if a claim is
ever brought.

## Third-priority blocker: military service portrayal

Per legal playbook §12 ("Military service / unit portrayals"):
- The author's own service (referenced in planning context as 82nd
  Airborne, Afghanistan, SAW gunner — not yet visible in Chapter 1)
  is his to tell.
- **Do not name real fellow service members and attribute fictional
  misconduct to them.** Defamation per se; very high exposure.
- **DOPSR review obligation.** If the author signed any NDA during
  service (SCI, special-access, classified briefings), there is a
  lifetime obligation under DoDI 5230.09 to submit writings drawing
  on protected information for prepublication review. **Check
  discharge paperwork and any NDAs before publication.** The DOPSR
  process is free and routine; the consequence of skipping it
  (*Snepp v. United States*) is suit to seize proceeds.

This is not a Chapter 1 issue but it is a publication-track issue.
Resolve before query stage.

## Scripture / religious-content considerations

Per legal playbook §12 and the implicit religious framework of the
book ("Architect of Grace," Diane's Church of Christ, etc.):

- **KJV scripture quotations are public domain.** Use freely.
- **NIV, ESV, NASB, NLT, etc. are under publisher copyright** with
  fair-use caps (typically 250–500 verses, not from a single book,
  not constituting a complete book of the Bible). If the manuscript
  quotes scripture, identify the translation and check the
  publisher's permission threshold. Quoting hymn lyrics from
  copyrighted hymnals (post-1928 generally) requires permission.
- **The "hymnals stolen in 1994" detail in Chapter 1 is fine** — it
  references hymnals as physical objects, not their text.

Per legal playbook §5, the Chapter 1 prose as written is registrable

registration form per the conservative path described in playbook
