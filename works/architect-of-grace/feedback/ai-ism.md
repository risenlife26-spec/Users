# AI-ism Pass — *The Architect of Grace*

*Scope: Chapter 1 partial. Per legal playbook §5, the author is using
Claude as an editorial assistant; this pass keeps the workflow defensible
by catching anything that drifts from his voice into generated-sounding
phrasing.*

## Overall: Chapter 1 reads as authored, not generated

The prose is too specific, too rooted in actual California geography, too
willing to repeat words ("pressing," "gate," "burning"), and too
committed to a single stylistic risk (long McCarthy-polysyndetic
sentences) to read as model output. AI-generated literary prose tends
toward smoothed parallel structure, abstract universals, hedged claims,
and an avoidance of repetition. Chapter 1 has none of these tells.

**The line that most clearly proves human authorship:** *"Cody could find
a bar in a blackout but couldn't find the Grapevine on a map."* This is a
joke that requires (a) knowing what a blackout drinker is, (b) knowing
the Grapevine as a specific stretch of road, (c) the rhythm to land it
short after a long sentence. Models don't make this joke unprompted; if
they do, they make it generic.

## Sentences to flag (low-to-medium AI-ism risk)

These are not necessarily bad — some may be the author's voice — but
they are the lines a hostile AI-detector or a USCO examiner would
question. Worth a re-read with the question: *did I write this from my
head, or did I accept a Claude suggestion?*

1. **"As if the burning had simplified it."**
   - Closing the scar paragraph.
   - Risk: philosophical-flourish gloss tacked onto physical detail.
     This is exactly the shape of an AI-suggested closer — it converts
     a concrete description into a meaning-bearing abstraction.
   - Verdict: keep only if "simplification by burning" becomes a
     recurring book motif. Otherwise cut.

2. **"The pressing was the only company he had."**
   - Closing the truck-shoulder section.
   - Risk: tidy parallel construction (pressed → pressing). Slightly
     over-balanced for the rough Blake-POV register established
     elsewhere.
   - Verdict: minor. Probably yours, but loosen the music.

3. **"Reno collected the absent fathers of the Central Valley like a
   drainage ditch collects runoff — passively, without effort, by
   being the lowest point."**
   - Risk: the **second half** ("passively, without effort, by being
     the lowest point") reads as a model expanding on its own
     metaphor. A human writer typically lets the simile land and moves
     on; a model often elaborates to "show its work."
   - Verdict: cut everything after "runoff."

4. **"He bent the world to fit himself and the world let him because
   the alternative was getting bent."**
   - Risk: the chiastic structure (bent / bent) is the kind of move
     models like. But this one is **earned** — it characterizes Mickey
     economically and the joke ("the alternative was getting bent")
     is specific.
   - Verdict: keep. This is yours. If it isn't, make it yours.

5. **"She believed in God the way she believed in the title company
   — as a structure that existed whether she understood it or not,
   that processed transactions she couldn't see, that would be there
   on Monday whether she showed up or not."**
   - Risk: the rule-of-three at the end ("…that…that…that…").
   - Verdict: keep. The metaphor is too specific to Diane's actual
     life (working at a *title company*) to be model-generated. The
     rule-of-three is earned. If you've trimmed any of those clauses
     to make the rhythm cleaner, restore the messier original.

## Patterns to watch in coming chapters

- **Smoothed parallel structure.** If a paragraph has three sentences
  starting with the same construction, ask whether the third is
  yours or a model completion.
- **Empty universals.** "There was something about him." "He had the
  kind of face that…" These are model placeholders for content the
  author hasn't actually generated. Replace with concrete fact.
- **Hedged claims.** "Perhaps." "Almost as if." "In a way." Models
  hedge; Blake's POV doesn't. Cut hedges.
- **Avoidance of repetition.** If you find yourself reaching for a
  synonym to avoid using "truck" twice, ask whether the model made
  you do that. Repetition is a working tool in your house.
