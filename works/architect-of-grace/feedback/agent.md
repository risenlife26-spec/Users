# Agent / Markets Pass — *The Architect of Grace*

*Scope: Chapter 1 partial. Comp and agent suggestions are starting points
to verify on Publishers Marketplace before any query.*

---

## Genre positioning

**Literary fiction** with the following more specific positioning options:

- **Country-noir / rural literary** — the David Joy / Daniel Woodrell /
  Larry Brown / Smith Henderson lane. Working-class American specificity,
  inherited masculinity, redemption-through-attrition. This is the
  closest fit based on Chapter 1's voice and material.
- **Western literary** — McCarthy / Larry McMurtry's quieter mode /
  Kent Haruf. Landscape as moral architecture; plain diction; stoic
  protagonists. The Central Valley setting and the Grapevine /
  Tehachapis / Bakersfield specificity puts the book in this lane
  geographically without committing to genre Western.
- **Christian-adjacent literary** — depending on how "Architect of
  Grace" plays out, the book may sit near Marilynne Robinson's
  Gilead novels or Christian Wiman's prose territory: theology that
  earns its place inside literary fiction without becoming
  evangelical-market work. This positioning would expand the
  publishable range significantly if the redemption arc is handled
  with genuine craft rather than message.

The risk to navigate: do **not** position this as Christian-market
fiction (CBA / ECPA) at the agent stage even if the redemption arc is
explicit. The Christian fiction market has different economics, lower
advances, and different agents — and the Chapter 1 voice (Marlboro
Reds, "the fuck are you doing out here, primo," "I'll never bitch and
that's a fact") will not pass CBA content standards. The book reads
as literary first, with theology as undercurrent. Position it that
way.

## Comp titles

The standard query letter wants two recent (last 3–5 years) comps
that signal genre lane and reader. From strongest to most
aspirational:

| Comp | Author | Why |
|---|---|---|
| *Where All Light Tends to Go* (2015) | David Joy | Working-class Appalachian son under inherited masculine pressure. Closest comp for character system. |
| *Fourth of July Creek* (2014) | Smith Henderson | Plain American voice with theological undercurrent. Closest comp for register. |
| *Days Without End* (2016) | Sebastian Barry | If the prose stays as long-sentenced and lyric as the Chapter 1 opening. |
| *Plainsong* (1999) | Kent Haruf | Older but still cited. Use only if the book's quietness deepens. |
| *Suttree* (1979) | Cormac McCarthy | Influence, not comp. Don't put it in the query letter — too presumptuous and too old. |

The query should pair *one* recent rural-literary comp (Joy or
Henderson) with one literary-establishment comp (Barry, or whatever
fits when more chapters exist).

## Query angle

Strongest pitch hook from Chapter 1:

> *Blake fixed. Cody replaced. Blake maintained. Cody burned. In
> Bakersfield, that was the difference between a man who survived
> what his uncle had built and a man who didn't.*

The book is being sold on:
- **Voice** — McCarthy lineage in plain register, located in the
  contemporary Central Valley
- **Inheritance** — what an absent father, a Vietnam-vet uncle, and
  a Teamster-with-connections leave a watching son
- **Redemption** — the "Architect of Grace" claim, which the query
  has to gesture toward without giving away the arc

What the query letter cannot lead with until later chapters exist:
- The actual plot
- The "Triptych in Four Movements" structural claim (sounds
  pretentious in a query without execution to back it up)
- The interludes (cite them as a structural element only if they're
  doing something distinctive)

## What needs to be ready before querying

Per the legal playbook §8 and §9, do not query without:
1. **A complete manuscript.** Agents do not sign debut literary
   novelists on partials.
2. **USCO Single Application filed** the day the manuscript leaves
   your household ($45). Per playbook §1–§2.
3. **Polished first 50 pages.** Most agents request first 10–50
   pages with the query. The agent will judge the book on these
   pages even more than on the synopsis.
4. **One-page synopsis.** Whole-book arc in a single page. Hardest
   document in the process.
5. **A cleaner sample of the title.** "*The Architect of Grace:
   Eternity's Fall — A Triptych in Four Movements*" is a lot. The
   query letter probably wants "*The Architect of Grace*" and the
   subtitle in the manuscript only.
