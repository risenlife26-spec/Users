# CHAPTER 16 // THE POSTCARD

## MANUSCRIPT

Blake found the postcard on a Sunday afternoon in December.

He was at the clinic. Not for an animal — for Lisa's things. The sign out front — KERN VALLEY VETERINARY — with the handwritten V still taped over the gap. The tape was yellowed. The V was holding.

Blake boxed the things. The stethoscope Lisa had bought with her own money. The textbook on canine orthopedics with the spine cracked at the chapter on cruciate repair. The framed diploma from UC Davis. The lab coat on the hook behind the door. The mug that said WORLD'S OKAYEST VET that someone had given her as a joke and that Lisa had used every day because Lisa did not waste things, not mugs, not jokes, not the breath in her lungs.

He opened the desk drawer.

In the back of the drawer, under a stack of lab order forms, Blake found the postcard.

It was from Reno. The front was a photograph of the Reno arch at night. The photograph was sun-faded. The postcard had been in the drawer for a long time.

Blake turned it over.

The handwriting was a man's handwriting — not careful, not careless, the handwriting of a person who had something to say and said it without revision because the saying was already costing more than the person could afford:

*Lisa — I know this isn't enough. I'm working at a place up here. Sending what I can when I can. I left your mother's fishing rods in the garage. Don't let Marcus touch them. They were your grandfather's. I'm sorry for more than I know how to put on a card. — Dad*

Blake read the postcard. He held the postcard. The fishing rods. The grandfather. The father who left. The postcard was the father's only letter. The only document. The only evidence that the man who had left knew that the leaving had cost something and the cost was more than he could put on a card.

Blake put the postcard in the box. He put the box in the truck. Titus was in the bed, watching the clinic door.

He drove to Fresno. He gave it to Margaret. Blake did not stay long. He drove back through the Valley — the 99 south, past the packing sheds, past the almond orchards in their cruciform rows. He took the White Lane exit. He drove south. Past the panadería. Past the tire shop. Past the clinic with the V taped to the sign. Past the gap between the streetlights. Past Salty's.

He pulled over. He sat in the truck with the engine running and the postcard's words in his head — *I'm sorry for more than I know how to put on a card.* Titus put his chin on the rail of the truck bed. His ears were soft. His eyes were on Blake through the rear window. The dog's face in the rearview mirror — the heavy jaw, the brown eyes, the expression of a creature that understood sorrow not as a concept but as a temperature, a weight, a change in the electrical charge of the air, and responded to it the only way the creature knew how: by staying.

Blake drove home.
