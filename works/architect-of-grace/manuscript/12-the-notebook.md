# CHAPTER 12 // THE NOTEBOOK

## MANUSCRIPT

Sarah stood in the bedroom. Blake's jeans from last night lay on the floor — dropped, not folded. She picked up the denim to fold it and felt the weight in the back pocket.

The notebook. Small. Spiral-bound. The cover soft from compression.

She opened it.

The handwriting was Blake's, but pressed hard, angled differently, the letters leaning forward.

*I come from pulling plungers back, to fill up my syringe of pain,*
*I come from selfish fathers dead and gone, yet they live a call away…*

Sarah sat on the edge of the bed. The notebook trembled in her manicured hands. The rigid architecture of her life — the color-coded accordion file, the meticulously curated dinner parties, the pristine Sancerre — shuddered against the raw, jagged lines of Blake's grief.

She turned the page.

*My hope is that the God of grace had seen your hurting heart,*
*and took you home to paradise as He did the thief hanging at the start.*

This was a man she did not know. A man weeping on paper, pleading with a God she considered a social utility. She pressed her thumb against the bloomed ink of the tear stain. Her chest seized. A desperate, suffocating tightness climbed her throat. She could not organize this. She could not box this away. The depth of his interiority mocked the shallow perimeter she had spent seven months enforcing.

She put the notebook back in the pocket. She folded the jeans with aggressive, mechanical precision, smoothing every crease, trying to assert order over a room that suddenly felt vast and terrifyingly empty.
