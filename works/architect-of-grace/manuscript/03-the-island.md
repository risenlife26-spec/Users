# CHAPTER 03 // THE ISLAND

## MANUSCRIPT

Blake was fourteen and Cody was sixteen and the plane banked over water so blue it looked like a lie.

Uncle Mickey had paid for everything. Mickey always paid for everything — flights, hotel, rental car, dinners — and he paid for them with cash from a roll in his front pocket that he peeled bills from with his thumb without looking at the denominations. Looking at the denominations would've implied the amount mattered, and nothing about money mattered to Mickey except that it proved he had it.

The market was in Lahaina. Open-air stalls. Tourist shells. Coconut oil and pineapple and the heavy sweet rot of plumeria. Blake was walking with his mother. Cody was ahead of them at a stall that sold puka shell necklaces — the white round kind.

Cody was six-one and still growing. He'd picked up a necklace and put it on. The shells sat against his collarbone and he was looking at himself in the small mirror. He was smiling. A kid's smile.

Mickey's shadow arrived behind Cody's shoulder in the mirror. A shift in the rhythm.

"The fuck is that."

Loud enough for the vendor. For Blake's mother.

Cody's hand went to the shells. "It's just a necklace."

"You look like a fucking faggot." Flat. Administrative. The tone of a man issuing a correction.

Cody took the necklace off. Set it on the counter. His jaw was working. His eyes were wet and he was fighting the wetness with everything he had.

Mickey looked at him. Looked at the necklace. Looked at the vendor, who was pretending to arrange shells.

"Pick it up and put it on. I want to see it again."

Cody picked up the necklace. He put it on. Blake watched from fourteen feet away.

Cody stood in the stall with the puka shells against his collarbone and looked at Mickey. The wetness was gone. The replacement had learned what the body could do.

Cody hit him. One punch. Right hand. Straight. No wind-up, no telegraph. The fist came from his hip like a piston from a cylinder and met the jaw of a fifty-one-year-old connected man at a velocity nobody in the market was prepared for.

Mickey's head snapped back. His body followed. He went down on the concrete and the sound of his skull hitting the ground was the sound of something that had been standing for a very long time finally being told to sit. Eyes closed. Mouth open.

The market went silent. The vendor behind his table.

Cody stood over his uncle. Puka shells still on. Right hand at his side, the knuckles already swelling. He looked at Blake. Two seconds.

Cody ran.

He went to a public beach two miles south where the tourists didn't go because the sand was rocky and the current was strong. He sat with his knees up and his arms around his shins and his swelling hand cradled against his chest and he watched the water.

He met a girl. Hawaiian. Brown skin, dark hair, younger by a year. She was sitting on the rocks with a beach towel that had a faded surfboard print. She didn't ask why he was there. Didn't ask about his hand. She sat next to him and they watched the water and after a while she said her name was Leilani and Cody said his name was Cody and that was the whole conversation for the first hour.

She came back the next morning with her brother's sleeping bag. She brought food from her mother's kitchen. She put her hand on his chest while she slept. Cody's heart was beating hard. When he shifted in the night, she adjusted her hand without waking. The adjusting was the staying and the staying was the thing.

On the fourth day, Cody came back. He walked into the hotel lobby in the same clothes he'd been wearing for four days — board shorts, a salt-stained T-shirt, the puka shell necklace still around his neck. Hand still swollen. He walked up to Mickey's room. Knocked.

Mickey opened the door. Drinking a Budweiser. His jaw was purple and yellow and swollen. His eyes were flat.

"I'm back," Cody said.

Mickey stared at him. Took a drink. Stepped back from the door, walked to the chair by the window, sat down. Didn't speak.

Cody walked in. Stood in front of Mickey. Didn't sit. Didn't apologize. The puka shells were on his neck and the swelling was on his hand.

Mickey finished his beer. Crushed the can. Looked at Cody.

"You got your mother's right hand."

That was all he said.
