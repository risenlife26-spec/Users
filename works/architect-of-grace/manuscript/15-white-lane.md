# CHAPTER 15 // WHITE LANE

## MANUSCRIPT

Sarah stood in the clinic waiting room, her fingers interlaced so tightly the joints turned white. The twenty-two-dollar candle, the Sancerre, the algorithm — none of it held the weight.

"I don't know who I am," Sarah whispered.

Lisa set the charger on the counter. "Sit down. Talk to me."

Sarah sat in the plastic chair. She spoke of her rigid spine, the endless organizing, the desperate need to control the air she breathed, and the terrifying realization of her own absolute emptiness.

Lisa leaned forward. "You're carrying so much, Sarah. You don't have to carry it alone."

Sarah's posture shattered.

The pupils dilated. The color left her face in a wave that moved from the forehead down. Lisa saw it the way she saw a crash in vitals — the numbers moving before the alarms. Sarah's mouth opened. Her hand came off the armrest. She stood.

The air in the clinic ruptured. The tile floor tilted. The sharp scent of chlorhexidine rushed upward. The fluorescent tube buzzed, a steady mechanical hum against the sudden impact of bone on linoleum.

The cold shock of the floor met Lisa's cheek. She did not reach for her chest. She did not brace for the fall. She lay on her side, watching Sarah's designer shoes stand motionless inches away. Lisa's right hand — the hand that had started IVs and drawn hearts for sixteen-year-old girls — rested flat against the tile, fingers spread wide.

Outside, the Civic's check-engine light glowed amber in the dark parking lot. The handwritten V held fast to the sign.
