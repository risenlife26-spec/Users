# CHAPTER 14 // ETERNITY OS

## MANUSCRIPT

Sarah built the algorithm on a Wednesday morning. She sat at her desk, her hands striking the keyboard with frantic precision. The spiral notebook burned in her memory — the ragged handwriting, the bleeding ink, the terrifying expanse of a husband she could not manage.

The chaos required a container. She built EternityOS. Not as a business venture, but as a psychological barricade. She coded an algorithm to scrape attendance logs, giving records, and volunteer hours, distilling the unstructured behavioral data into a single, color-coded "spiritual health score." Green, yellow, red. If faith could be quantified, if grief could be dashboarded, the terrifying grace Blake had found on the grow room floor could be tamed.

She typed until her wrists ached, converting the raw, ungovernable mess of human sorrow into sterile, predictable data points, building a digital fortress to block out the silence of her own home.

EternityOS could read giving records and attendance logs and sermon-download statistics. The system recognized: OPTIMAL, SUBOPTIMAL, SUSTAINABLE, UNSUSTAINABLE. Cody Reyes had been filed under UNSUSTAINABLE. The death reclassified him to RESOLVED. The algorithm worked perfectly, right up until the moment it encountered grace. Grace refuses the grid. Grace is unstructured behavioral data, and it is going to break the hard drive.
